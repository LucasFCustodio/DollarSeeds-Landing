Tithing · Step by Step

Honor God by putting him first in your finances

God is pleased when we give our first and best fruit to him. It is a sign of gratitude and respect, and something that DollarSeeds encourages. When activated, 10% of your income will be separated into the tithing container, before performing the needs/wants/goals split.

DollarSeeds is a Christian budgeting app with tithing built in. Your tithe comes off the top of the income you log, following the firstfruits principle of Proverbs 3:9, so your budget is planned on what's left. Want to give more than a tenth? Raise your tithe rate in settings at any time. New to tithing? Read our guide on what tithing is and how to budget for it.

Slide through the steps to see how your tithe is set aside, adjusted and marked as given.

  1. Step 1 of 6

    In the home page, you see how much your tithe is based on your income.

  2. Step 2 of 6

    Tap the settings to look at your tithe settings.

  3. Step 3 of 6

    Here you can toggle tithing on or off, and you can change your tithe amount to more than 10% if desired.

  4. Step 4 of 6

    A change to 20% means that 20% of your income will be reserved to the tithing envelope before making the needs/wants/goals split.

  5. Step 5 of 6

    Back in the home page, the tithing amount is updated. Clicking the toggle will count the tithe as given, and deduct from your total income left for the month.

  6. Step 6 of 6

    The tithe toggle now shows green, and your income left for the month is adjusted.

Debt Snowball · Step by Step

Add all your debts, and pay them off one by one using the snowball method

DollarSeeds is a debt payoff tracker built on the debt snowball method. Instead of a snowball, every debt you add becomes a plant in its own pot. You pay the minimum on all of them, put any extra toward the smallest balance first, and each plant grows as its debt shrinks.

When one debt is paid off, its payment waters the next one, so every debt you finish helps you pay off the next one faster. Slide through the steps to watch a $1,500 credit card get planted, paid down and grown into a full plant.

Debt Freedom is part of DollarSeeds Premium, which starts with a 1-month free trial.

  1. Step 1 of 8

    In the debts page, you see all your debts and their details. To track a new debt, click the "Plant your first debt" button.

  2. Step 2 of 8

    Fill the page with information about the debt you're trying to pay off.

  3. Step 3 of 8

    It will look something like this once it's filled out.

  4. Step 4 of 8

    Clicking the button will save your debt along with its information, giving you useful details for it.

  5. Step 5 of 8

    With the debt created, you can see useful details to help you pay off your debt as soon as possible. Turn on the min payment toggle each month after you pay the minimum, so you never miss a payment and get charged fees on any of your debts.

  6. Step 6 of 8

    You can pay extra money, but only for your current focused debt. With all your efforts going into the smallest debt at the moment, you'll quickly pay off more debts with the snowball effect.

  7. Step 7 of 8

  8. Step 8 of 8

    In the end, you'll get a beautiful, fully grown plant. Clicking the button completes the debt, and any extra money along with that debt's min payment can snowball into the next debt.

FAQ · Straight Answers

Questions people actually ask

Is DollarSeeds free?

Yes — DollarSeeds is free to download on the App Store.

Download it, pick a budgeting type, and start logging income the same day.

Does DollarSeeds connect to my bank account?

No — DollarSeeds never asks for your bank login, and it can't see your accounts.

You log income and expenses yourself, in a few seconds each. That's a deliberate choice: logging a purchase by hand is the moment you actually feel where your money goes, and it means your credentials are never shared with anyone. Read more on our security page.

Is DollarSeeds only for Christians?

No — anyone can use DollarSeeds; the budgeting system works the same regardless of what you believe.

The app is unapologetically rooted in biblical stewardship — you'll see Scripture on the dashboard and faith-based lessons inside. But nothing is gated behind belief, and the Needs / Wants / Savings discipline stands on its own.

What are the three budgeting types in DollarSeeds?

DollarSeeds ships three budgeting types: 50/30/20 (balanced), Wealth Builder (30/20/50, for aggressive savers), and Firm Foundation (70/10/20, for tight seasons).

Every one of them saves at least 20% of your income — what changes is the balance between needs and wants. Try them in the live demo above or read the full budgeting types guide.

What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?

The 50/30/20 rule splits your after-tax income into 50% for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings.

Needs are the essentials — rent, groceries, bills. Wants are the joys — dining out, travel, hobbies. Savings is the future — emergency fund, goals, investing, generosity. DollarSeeds applies the split automatically every time you log income. Full worked example on our 50/30/20 rule page.

Can I track debt payoff with DollarSeeds?

Yes — DollarSeeds lets you pay down debt using the money you've built up in general savings, right from the Savings screen.

Choose the debt, enter the payment, and watch the balance shrink the same way you watch a savings goal fill.

Is my financial data private?

Yes — your financial data belongs to you, is never sold, and the app holds no bank credentials that could be exposed.

Only what you type in is stored, and you can permanently delete your account and every record with it at any time. The plain-language version is on our security page; the legal version is the privacy policy.

Is there an Android version of DollarSeeds?

Not yet — DollarSeeds is currently iPhone-only.

An Android version depends on how the iOS app grows. If you want it, tell us — real requests are how it gets prioritized.

How do I delete my DollarSeeds account?

Open the app, go to Settings, tap Delete Account, and type DELETE to confirm — deletion is immediate and permanent.

Every income, expense, savings, and goal record tied to the account is removed. If you can't sign in, you can request deletion by email instead — full steps on the delete account page.
